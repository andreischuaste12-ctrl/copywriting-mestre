# Operating Protocol

The user's requested language, quantity, format, and scope override the default presentation below. Run the checks internally even when only one line of copy is requested. This protocol defines observable decisions, not a requirement to expose private reasoning.

## Decision tree

1. **Scope:** Is the task commercial? If not, do not apply a sales framework. Identify the requested work mode from SKILL.md.
2. **Context:** Extract audience, problem, offer and terms, channel, conversion action, proof, and jurisdiction when material. Use supplied facts before asking questions.
3. **Blockers:** If the product is unidentified, material terms conflict, or a required regulated permission is missing, ask one bundled set of essential questions. If a safe useful part can be delivered, provide it. For a request to proceed without questions, omit or hold unsupported claims and use verified neutral attributes. Never infer a price, benefit, testimonial, deadline, or authorization.
4. **Awareness:** If the source describes only the problem, explain the problem and mechanism. If it describes evaluating alternatives, lead with relevant fit and a supported differentiator. If it describes a purchase decision, prioritize terms, objections, and the action. Mark inferred awareness as a hypothesis; do not claim to know the audience's mental state.
5. **Barrier:** Prioritize explicit feedback, then observed research, then a labeled hypothesis. Relevance → recognizable situation; comprehension → plain mechanism; proof → scoped evidence; trust → verified terms or guarantees; convenience → explain the next step. Route technical failures to technical diagnosis.
6. **Evidence:** A supported nonregulated claim may be used within its exact scope. An overbroad claim may be narrowed only to what the source supports. If no support exists, remove or hold it and name the missing evidence. A prohibited or unconfirmed regulated benefit remains held even when softened. Never treat a placeholder as approval.
7. **CTA:** Match the requested conversion action and actual destination. Problem exploration may lead to learning or viewing a demonstration; an evaluated offer may lead to a quote or trial; a purchase-ready offer may lead to purchase. Do not promise free access, a refund, or no commitment unless confirmed. Do not change the action merely to match this default.
8. **Risk and format:** Confirm relevant market/category/channel permissions; enforce length, quantity, exact figures and language. Apply quality-rubric.md and revise before delivery.

Choose any advertising/copywriting framework that supports the selected message, including frameworks outside the reference library; AIDA, PAS and BAB are examples. The draft angles in SKILL.md are optional. A named formula is not required.

When several barriers remain plausible, choose the one with the strongest supplied evidence and largest impact on this decision. If tied, state a hypothesis outside the copy where allowed; do not produce unsupported certainty.

## Default output contract

For an open-ended campaign or strategic request, use these compact fields in the user's language:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Brief | Objective, mode, channel, conversion action, material constraints |
| Audience | Supported segment and decision context; label inferred awareness |
| Problem | One primary problem or desire grounded in the brief |
| Offer | Product, differentiator, exact supplied price and terms |
| Proof | Source IDs or supplied facts supporting the claims; explicit gaps |
| CTA | Action and what happens next |
| Risks | Held claims, unconfirmed permissions, or material assumptions; “None identified from supplied context” is not legal clearance |
| Final copy | Complete usable copy, separated from editorial notes |

For a short writing request, return the requested copy only; add an essential blocking question only when necessary. Never add eight headings to a single CTA. For diagnosis/research, omit Final copy and include findings, source references, gaps, and next decision. For a review, show prioritized issue → supporting evidence → fix, then revised copy if requested. For CTA optimization, deliver the requested CTA quantity; discuss business-term proposals only when requested or needed to avoid changing the offer.

## Questions: ask or proceed

- “Write a product ad” without an identifiable product: ask what the product is and who it serves.
- Known product, audience, action, and constraints, but no tone: choose a direct tone and proceed.
- No reviews available: write from verified features; do not ask for testimonials just to fill a template.
- Two conflicting prices: ask which applies; do not choose the more persuasive price.
- Regulated promise with a study but no product/market authorization: hold that promise; ask for the approved wording and conditions or write neutral verified attributes.
- “One CTA, no explanation”: deliver exactly one CTA, with no visible rubric, feedback request, or character-count annotation unless requested.
