# Claim Review Matrix

Use this worksheet when a campaign includes multiple benefits, numbers, comparisons, studies, guarantees, or regulated claims. A factual source does not by itself grant permission to advertise a claim.

| Draft claim | Claim type | Evidence and source | What the evidence actually supports | Market / channel | Authorization confirmed by | Conditions / qualifications | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Exact wording] | [Fact, estimate, testimonial, comparison, regulated] | [Document, date, owner] | [Scope, population, method, limits] | [Country, category, channel] | [Official rule, label, legal review] | [Eligibility, timeframe, exclusions] | [Use, revise, hold] |

## Review sequence

1. Preserve the exact proposed wording and classify its material meaning.
2. Identify a responsible source. Record its date, population, context, method, and limitations.
3. Check that the evidence supports the whole claim, including implied outcomes, comparison, and certainty.
4. Separately confirm authorization for the product, category, market, audience, and advertising channel.
5. Add material conditions where the decision occurs. Do not rely on fine print to reverse a headline impression.
6. If either evidence or authorization is missing, hold the claim. Ask for the source or use neutral, verifiable product attributes that do not depend on it.

A softer wording is not automatically safe if it still communicates a prohibited or unsupported benefit. Legal review and business approval remain with the responsible organization.
