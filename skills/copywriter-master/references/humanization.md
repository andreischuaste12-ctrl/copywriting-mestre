# Humanizing Copy

Use these as editorial principles, not a template that overrides the brief, channel, or brand voice.

- Use a concrete opening situation or detail when factual context supports it. Do not invent names, places, figures, or scenes to appear specific.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length. Short sentences can create pace; longer ones can explain. Do not force mechanical word counts.
- Prefer the audience's words and specific names to generic nouns. Read aloud and remove lines that sound like marketing written for other marketers.
- Do not add signposted conclusions, repeated summaries, disguised lists, or generic motivational endings when a concrete final point works better.
- Avoid figurative jargon such as leverage, optimize, empower, unlock, accelerate, synergy, paradigm, journey, beacon, ecosystem, and landscape. Name the actual action, object, or outcome.
- Acknowledge real caveats, costs, and limits. Do not fake mistakes, vulnerability, or intimacy to manufacture a human voice.
- Preserve the brief's facts and intent. Humanizing means making the message natural and specific; it does not authorize exaggeration.

## Quick review

Check for a context-supported detail, natural rhythm, familiar words, a clear position, and a useful ending. If style rules make the copy sound artificial, prioritize natural reading and the requested format.
