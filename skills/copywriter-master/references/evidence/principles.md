# Evidence principles: how to use research in copy

Persuasion research gives hypotheses, not formulas. Average effects of message techniques are small and vary widely across audiences, channels and barriers (see [effect-sizes-and-testing.md](effect-sizes-and-testing.md)). The diagnosed barrier and the reader's awareness choose the structure; a named technique never does.

## Four tiers of authority

Say which tier supports a recommendation when the distinction matters, and never present a lower tier as a higher one.

| Tier | What it is | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Empirical evidence | Replicated findings about cognition, perception and behavior | Highest, within the conditions studied |
| 2. Explanatory theory | Models of how information is processed | Contextual: it explains, it does not prove an effect in this campaign |
| 3. Professional heuristic | Accumulated direct-response practice, including the [copywriter profiles](../copywriters/index.md) | A tool to try, with no universal truth value |
| 4. Style preference | Brand voice, taste, aesthetics | Subjective and variable |

## Rules for using the evidence

1. **Precedence.** Brief fidelity, claim integrity and compliance in SKILL.md and the [jurisdiction packs](../jurisdictions/brazil.md) override every evidence rule. If a rule below would need an invented figure, urgency, story or testimonial, do not apply it.
2. **Condition first.** Each rule is "if this situation, then try this". Check that the condition is true for this brief before applying it.
3. **Justify by barrier.** Explain a choice by the diagnosed barrier (relevance, comprehension, proof, trust, convenience). Do not write "this converts because it uses AIDA" or "because of loss aversion".
4. **Propose a test** when a decision rests on a hypothesis: name the hypothesis, metric and guardrail ([optimization-and-testing.md](../optimization-and-testing.md)).
5. **Quote only what is printed here.** Several figures that circulate in practitioner material are not supported by their supposed sources; those entries say so. Do not add numbers from memory.
6. **Transfer is an inference.** Most studies were not run on commercial copy. The entries mark transfer as adjacent or extrapolated.

## Citation status

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| supplied | Bibliographic data comes from the supplied base and was not independently checked |
| index-checked | The record matched a secondary index; the full text was not read |
| corrected | The supplied data was wrong and was corrected from an index record |
| confirm-before-quoting | A related work named for context; confirm it against the source before citing |

None of these papers was read in full by the maintainers. Treat summaries as orientation, not as a replacement for the source.
